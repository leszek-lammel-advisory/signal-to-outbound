# From signal to message

A signal only helps if the message explains why it matters to the reader. Most signal-based copy fails because it names the signal and then pitches the product, with nothing in between.

- [signal-to-message-prompt.md](signal-to-message-prompt.md): a prompt that teaches your AI to do this for your product.

## The bridge

Before writing anything, finish this sentence:

> **[Signal]** usually means **[what changes for them]**, and **[the part of my product]** helps with exactly that.

If you can't finish it honestly, skip the company. That's a valid outcome, not a failure.

| Signal | What changes for them | Product | The part that fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDR job posted for DACH | A new rep needs months to book meetings, and the manager onboards them | SDR ramp program | AI role-play built from their ICP, so the manager isn't the trainer |
| New Head of Finance, 2 months in | Reviewing every vendor and process they inherited | Spend management tool | A clean view of what they now own, in week one |
| First job post in a new country | No pipeline, no local contacts, no brand there | Outbound agency | Meetings with local buyers before the hire starts |
| Job post names "Salesforce migration" | A project with an owner and a deadline | Data migration tool | Moving the data without a consultant team |
| Uses a competitor that just raised prices | Someone will be asked to justify the new invoice | Competing product | A side-by-side cost for their size |
| Funding round, no other signal | More money, no specific problem yet | Almost anything | Weak bridge: treat as a touchpoint or skip |

The middle column is where your AI does the real work. It's an inference, so the message says it carefully: "usually", "often", "is that the case?".

## Three frameworks

### 1. Two lines and a question

From Mateusz's part of the session. Best for LinkedIn and short first emails.

- **Line 1:** the signal, specific enough that they recognise it.
- **Line 2:** what it likely means for them, and how you help.
- **Question:** one direct question they can answer with yes, no or a name.

How you write line 2 depends on whether the signal is a pitch or a touchpoint ([what's the difference](../signals/README.md#pitch-or-touchpoint)).

**Pitch** (the signal is itself a reason to buy):

> Hi {first name}, DataGuard has an SDR and a BDR role open in Munich. Two new reps usually means two people booking very little for the first months. Is the ramp plan for them already set?

**Touchpoint** (the signal is a reason to talk, not proof of need):

> Hi {first name}, saw the Series A you announced in October. After a round, the plan often includes the first sales hires. Is outbound on it for next year, or still run by the founders?

### 2. Observation, inference, question

For signals where the meaning isn't obvious: tech changes, expansions, content they posted.

1. **Observation:** a fact with a date or a place. "Your Paris office opened in October."
2. **Inference:** what it usually leads to, said carefully. "New offices usually start with zero local pipeline."
3. **Question:** about that consequence, not about your product. "Who's building the first customer list there?"

The inference is where you show you understand their world. If it's wrong, the question lets them correct you, and that's still a reply.

### 3. Saw, we help, worth sending over

For cold email with a useful asset instead of a meeting ask. From our public method, [lammel-copywriting](https://github.com/leszek-lammel-advisory/lammel-copywriting).

```text
Hi {first name},

Saw {one specific observation}.

We help {type of company} {specific outcome}. {Customer} used {product} to {verified result}.

Worth sending over {a specific useful asset}?
```

Under 80 words. The asset must be something they'd want even if they never buy: a sample, a comparison of two real options, a worked example of their situation. "A tailored blueprint" doesn't count.

## Rules for every message

1. **Name the event and when or where.** "Saw you're growing" is not a signal. "Saw the SDR DACH role you posted last week" is.
2. **One signal per message.** Stacking three signals reads like surveillance.
3. **Say inferences carefully.** You know they posted a job. You don't know they're struggling.
4. **No invented facts, numbers or customer results.** If you don't have proof, leave it out.
5. **Don't open with congratulations.** Every vendor does it the day after a funding round.
6. **Short.** Under 50 words on LinkedIn, under 80 in email.

## The swap test

Run this on every message before you use it:

1. **Swap in another company with the same signal.** Does the message still work? Good: you have a template that scales.
2. **Swap in a company without the signal.** Does it still work? Then the signal is decoration. Rewrite line 2 until it depends on the signal.
